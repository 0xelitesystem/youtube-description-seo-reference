# The description structure

Structure a YouTube description with the most important information first: a clear, specific summary of what the video covers in the opening lines, then chapters, then links and context. The first lines carry the most weight for both viewers and search, because that is what shows before anyone clicks to expand.

## The first lines do the work

Only the first lines of a description show before a viewer expands it, and those lines carry disproportionate weight for search and discovery. Lead with a specific, plain summary of what the video actually covers, using the words people would search for, rather than a generic greeting or a wall of links. The opening is the description's headline, and it should earn the click and signal the topic clearly.

## Then the structure

After the opening summary, give the description structure: a brief expansion of the topics covered, then time-stamped chapters, then relevant links, then any standing context like who you are and where else to find you. This ordering puts what matters for discovery up top and the housekeeping below. A structured description is easier for both viewers and engines to parse than an undifferentiated block.

## Use the real search language

Throughout the description, use the actual words and phrases people would type to find the video, naturally and without stuffing. Search and discovery match queries against the description, so describing the content in the language of the people looking for it helps them find it. The goal is an honest, specific description that happens to use the terms your audience searches, not a list of keywords jammed in.

## Avoid the generic opener

The common mistake is opening with a generic greeting, a subscribe plea, or a block of links before any description of the content. This wastes the most valuable lines on something that does not help discovery and does not tell anyone what the video is. Save the calls to action and links for after the summary. The opening lines are too valuable for boilerplate; spend them on a specific description of the video.
