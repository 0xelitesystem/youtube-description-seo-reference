# The companion post

Pair each video with a companion blog post on your own site that contains the full transcript, a written summary, key takeaways, and time-stamped chapters. The post turns the video's content into a page that Google and AI engines can index, extract, and cite, which a video alone cannot offer them.

## Why video needs a text companion

Search and AI engines extract and cite text far more readily than they parse video. A video on its own gives them little to index beyond its metadata. A companion post containing the video's actual content in text form gives engines something to read, quote, and cite, multiplying where the content can be found. The post is how video content enters the text-based discovery and citation systems.

## What the post contains

A strong companion post embeds the video, then provides a written summary of what it covers, a list of key takeaways, the full transcript, and time-stamped chapters linking into the video. This gives a reader the content in skimmable text and gives engines a rich, extractable page. The post is not a teaser; it is the video's content rendered as a citable page, with the video itself embedded for those who prefer to watch.

## Owned and discoverable

The companion post lives on your own site, which you control, rather than depending solely on the video platform. This means the content is discoverable through your site's search presence and is not subject only to one platform's algorithm. Owning the text version of your video content, on a property you control, is a hedge against platform dependence and a second front for discovery. The video reaches viewers; the post reaches searchers and engines.

## The link both ways

Connect the two: link from the video, including a pinned comment and the description, to the companion post, and embed the video in the post. This sends viewers who want the text to the post and sends readers who want to watch to the video, while signaling the relationship to engines. The cross-linking makes the pair work as a unit, each surface feeding the other, rather than as two disconnected pieces of content.
